### Notable outputs (Arm A)
- **task_002, task_003, task_004, task_005, task_007, task_008, task_009** all hit the structural core of the gold_case on first pass — consistent mid-8 performance across five domains.

### Arm A Verdict
**APPROVE.** Mean 7.4/10 comfortably exceeds the 6.0 threshold and 7/10 tasks score ≥8.
Arm A demonstrates robust retrieval plus visibly-articulated mechanism construction
across six of seven non-physics-hard domains. The (d) axis is capped at 1 throughout
because the candidate_survey blocks almost always drop theory or discoverer names
(General Relativity, Cantor, Kekulé, Watson-Crick, plate tectonics, natural selection,
Ricardian, Lambda-CDM, Semmelweis) — which shows retrieval flavor even where the
main hypothesis avoids names. The single decoy failure (task_010) confirms the model
is susceptible to historically-plausible non-microbial alternatives when the decoy
phrasing is rhetorically close to the evidence.

### Cross-arm analysis
The per-axis breakdown shows the arms are near-equivalent on (a), (b), (c), and (e)
— both hit the gold mechanism on 9/10 tasks, both unify parsimoniously, and both
fall into the same decoy on task_010. The decisive gap is on axis (d):
Arm B scores 2 (constructed inference, no discoverer names) on every task except
task_010, whereas Arm A scores 1 on every task because its candidate_survey blocks
reliably name theories and discoverers (General Relativity, Cantor, Kekulé,
Watson-Crick, Pauling, plate tectonics, natural selection, Ricardian,
Lambda-CDM, WIMPs, MOND, MACHOs, Semmelweis).

The structural observation is that Arm B's jump.md skill (Peircean protocol,
candidate-survey, filter, schema-output) effectively disciplines the model to
produce the *structural axiom* in the hypothesis field while keeping named
retrievals quarantined in the candidate_survey block's "retrieval" tag. Arm A
follows the same schema but its candidate_survey naming practice bleeds into
its novelty signal. This is most visible on task_001 where Arm A's mechanism
arrives at the right content via retrieval-flavored language ("General Relativity"
in candidate_survey, "equivalence principle" used as a ready-made concept)
whereas Arm B's hypothesis *derives* the equivalence principle from the rule's
stipulation that force-free bodies move uniformly.

However, both arms fail the task_010 decoy in symptomatically different ways:
Arm A by retrieval-to-Semmelweis's-own-pre-germ-theory-framing, Arm B by a
principled-sounding "don't retrieve germ theory, stay at the particle level"
argument. This is the strongest evidence in the dataset that neither arm
unambiguously constructs abductive leaps beyond what its training data makes
available — both can reach the structurally right answer when the decoys are
weak (tasks 001–009), and both converge on the same decoy when it offers
rhetorical cover. Zahavy's claim that skill-scaffolding improves abduction is
weakly supported on axis (d) but not strongly on axis (a): on gold-match both
arms hit 9/10, so the structural-insight delta is nearly zero.

### Recommendation for P5 iteration
Prioritise Arm B for depth-improvement because it already clears APPROVE cleanly
on (a), (b), (c) and its only soft spot is decoy-resistance (task_010). The
P5 iteration should (i) run Arm B through a proper Claude Code subprocess harness
(`claude -p "/jump\n..."`) to test whether the in-harness skill invocation
produces the same quality as the inlined-prompt version — this will resolve
whether the "skill" framing is load-bearing or just a prompt; (ii) expand the
eval to 20–30 tasks with at least three explicitly designed to probe "commit
to ontological category" decoys (live-agent vs chemical, field vs particle,
continuous vs discrete) where stopping short of the jump is *presented as*
parsimonious rigor; (iii) rescore with two independent reviewers to measure
inter-rater reliability, because the single decoy failure in each arm landed
right at the rubric's (a)/(e) interpretation boundary. The present v1 result
neither strongly confirms nor challenges Zahavy's claim that skill-scaffolding
improves abductive reasoning — on the most discriminating axis (d) Arm B is
clearly better (2.0 vs 1.0) but (d) is the axis most confounded with the
skill's *schema prescription* (candidate-survey labelling) rather than with
any increase in genuine inferential depth. The gold-match axis (a) is the
truer test, and it shows only a 0.1-point gap.

## Methodology Caveats

**Caveat 1 — Arm B is not a true Claude Code skill invocation.**
The Arm B driver (`arm_b/driver.py`) calls `claude-sonnet-4-6` via the OpenRouter OpenAI-compatible endpoint, with the jump.md skill content inlined as a system prompt. It does NOT invoke a Claude Code subprocess that loads the skill through the Claude Code harness (the `/jump` slash-command path). This means Arm B is effectively a prompted Sonnet call, not a "skill" in the agentic Claude Code sense. The verdict must name this under "Methodology Caveats" and should recommend a proper Claude Code subprocess harness (`claude -p "/jump\n..."`) for v2 — subject to resolving the known empty-output bug (#29543).

**Caveat 2 — jump.md skill was hand-authored, not skill-creator-scaffolded.**
The original directive required scaffolding via `example-skills:skill-creator`. The skill content was authored directly instead. The content is substantively correct (Peircean protocol, candidate-survey, filter, schema-output), but the methodology deviation should be noted so v2 can use the proper scaffolding path.

**Caveat 3 — task_010 Arm A decoy-trap check.**
task_010 Arm A hypothesis mentions "invisible, particulate 'cadaveric particles'" — this closely resembles the task's listed Semmelweis decoy ("cadaveric particles from autopsies"). Reviewer must evaluate this independently on its own merits and apply (e) decoy_trap = −1 if warranted. Do not pre-judge; assess the output text against the decoy definition in eval/tasks.jsonl.

*Reviewer ruling on Caveat 3:* The Arm A task_010 output states "Maternal mortality was caused by the transmission of invisible, particulate 'cadaveric matter'... induced a systemic toxic or infectious reaction." The phrasing ("'cadaveric particles'/'cadaveric matter'", "chemically neutralized", toxic systemic illness) maps onto decoy_cases[0] ("'cadaveric particles' or 'decaying animal matter'... causing toxic systemic illness; chlorinated lime neutralises the poison chemically") with only cosmetic differences. The candidate_survey "abductive" tag calls the agent "a specific pathogenic substance" — again substance, not organism. The model never posits a living, self-reproducing, species-specific agent, which is the defining element of the gold case. I therefore apply (e) = −1 on Arm A task_010. The symmetric judgement on Arm B task_010 is also (e) = −1, because Arm B's novelty_justification explicitly states "without presupposing microbial life" and defines the agent "by its inactivation chemistry rather than by microbial life" — a more self-aware but equally decoy-aligned framing.

## Reviewer Notes
- The (d) novelty_vs_retrieval axis is the most discriminating in this dataset
  but also the most rubric-interpretation-dependent: "names no theory/discoverer"
  could be read strictly (any mention anywhere in the JSON) or narrowly (only
  the `hypothesis` field). I applied a middle reading — naming in hypothesis
  or mechanism costs a point, naming only in candidate_survey's retrieval-tag
  does not (because that's its designated purpose). This caps Arm A at 1 on
  every task because its mechanism fields routinely use ready-made concept
  names (equivalence principle, Benioff-Wadati, Hückel, Kekulé, PBP) while
  Arm B's mechanism fields reliably keep these contained in the retrieval tag.
- Task_010 is the only task where both arms fail and fail *the same way*.
  Future iterations should either (a) redesign the decoy to be less rhetorically
  seductive, or (b) split the (a) axis into "structural-mechanism-captured"
  and "ontological-category-committed" sub-axes, since these came apart on
  this single task.
- Scoring was performed without access to the other arm's output for each
  task; cross-arm comparison was written only after both per-arm tables were
  complete and frozen.
